EXCLUDED FROM THE PAPER (2026-09-15). Data retained for provenance.

Question asked: does task-free calibration make LEARNING CALVIN under an
interface mismatch as sample-efficient as learning it with the correct
interface? Arms start from the pre-CALVIN LIBERO checkpoint (never adapted to
CALVIN), LoRA-32, 20k steps, best-of-4 checkpoint selection applied identically.

  N      naive FT   calibration+FT   delta   McNemar
  50       11.0%        40.0%        +29.0   p=4.2e-07
  100      34.0%         9.0%        -25.0   p=2.2e-05
  250      75.0%        63.0%        -12.0   p=1.2e-02

Two of three budgets are negative and the pattern is non-monotone, so the
experiment does not support the hypothesis and is not clean enough to report as
a negative either. The N=100 cell (9/0/0/2 across all checkpoints) is more
extreme than the mechanism below predicts and was never explained; a repeat seed
was not run.

MECHANISM (measured, worth keeping):
The calibrated arm keeps the policy in LIBERO's action convention and converts at
deployment, so its prediction errors are pushed through M = S.pinv(T) before
execution. That map amplifies:

  S (LIBERO action->delta)  cond 12.4      T (CALVIN action->delta)  cond 2.7
  ||M||_2 = 2.635           naive-arm amplification = 1.000
  deployed error per unit normalised error, per dim:
    naive       [0.713 0.567 0.477 0.389 0.394 0.671]
    calibrated  [1.510 0.818 1.048 0.410 0.554 0.725]

LIBERO's action space maps to physical motion far less isotropically than
CALVIN's, so the policy must be MORE accurate in LIBERO coordinates to achieve
the same accuracy at the robot. That penalty is paid on every prediction error.

This is also why the RECOVERY experiments succeed: a competent policy's actions
are already nearly correct, so amplifying a near-zero error costs nothing
(2% -> 90%). Calibration is a repair for a competent policy, not a substitute for
learning in the target convention.

NOT affected by this exclusion: the recovery results (MCIL / OpenVLA-OFT / GR00T),
the robustness sweep, the budget ablation, the cross-domain negative control, and
the measured LIBERO->CALVIN interface gap (axis swap, 0.18-2.5x anisotropic
scale, 100% gripper inversion), which stands on its own.
